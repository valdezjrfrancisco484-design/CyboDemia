Learn Cybersecurity

What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting systems, networks, and data from digital attacks, unauthorized access, and harm.

Learning and practicing cybersecurity can help individuals better understand and defend against digital risks. It is essential in protecting personal information, devices, and systems from threats such as hacking, fraud, and data breaches. As more aspects of daily life move online such as communication, education, banking, shopping, and entertainment, cybersecurity becomes increasingly important in ensuring privacy, safety, and trust in digital interactions. It also plays a critical role in preventing cybercrimes and safeguarding sensitive data in a highly connected world. Furthermore, cybersecurity promotes responsible use of technology for individuals, organizations, and society as a whole.

Online Safety and Security

Staying safe online is an important part of using the internet responsibly. These simple cybersecurity practices help protect your personal information, prevent cyber threats, and reduce the risk of scams and data breaches. Following these tips can greatly improve your digital safety in everyday online activities.

  • Use strong, unique passwords
  • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA)
  • Avoid clicking suspicious links or attachments
  • Be careful with sharing personal information
  • Keep devices and apps updated
  • Use secure websites and log out on shared devices

    What are Cyber Threats?

    Cyber threats are harmful activities carried out through digital systems that aim to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to data, devices, and networks.

    Digital threats can take many forms, such as phishing, malware, hacking, and online scams, and they continue to evolve as technology advances. As individuals increasingly rely on digital platforms for communication, education, and daily tasks, understanding cyber threats is essential in recognizing risks and preventing potential harm. Being aware of these threats helps users take appropriate precautions and make safer decisions when navigating the digital world.

    Below are some of the most common types of cyber threats:

  • Phishing - Fake messages or websites used to trick users into giving personal information.
  • Malware - Harmful software designed to damage or disrupt devices and systems.
  • Ransomware - A type of malware that locks data or systems and demands payment to restore access.
  • Spyware - Software that secretly monitors and collects user information without consent.
  • Hacking - Unauthorized access to systems or accounts to steal, alter, or destroy data.
  • Data Breaches - Exposure or theft of sensitive information from a system or database.
  • Identity Theft - Stealing personal information to impersonate someone for fraud or misuse.

    • Cyber threats continue to evolve as technology advances, making awareness and understanding more important than ever. By recognizing these threats, individuals can take the necessary steps to protect themselves and stay safe in the digital world.
